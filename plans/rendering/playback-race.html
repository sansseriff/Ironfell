Why playback stutters when the two frames finish together

Chrome, 6K window, the demo square playing by itself (no mouse). The hold is not acting in any of this; section 3 shows why.

1. The rule: one draw per vsync, and it takes whatever worker frame is already there

The compositor draws when the page's own frame arrives (green dot, ~5.9 ms after vsync here). It does not wait for the worker. The worker's tick for frame k starts at vsync k and finishes ~6.5 ms later (magenta). If the worker frame lands before the draw, that draw shows frame k. If it lands after, the draw shows the previous frame again, and frame k waits for the next draw, where it is usually replaced by frame k+1 before anyone sees it.

Both times wobble by about ±0.5 ms from frame to frame. Because they are nearly equal, the wobble alone decides the order, so it flips at random. The traces counted 120–167 flips in 7 s on stuttering runs, and 1 on a smooth one.

2. Early every time or late every time is smooth. Flipping is the stutter

Square position on screen, one mark per displayed frame. A late-every-time worker is just one frame behind, evenly. A flip from early to late shows the same position twice (a repeat), and a flip back jumps two steps (a double). That is exactly what the probe counts.

3. Why the hold doesn't fix it (or cause it) here

Your intuition is right: if the hold could run, it would force "early every time" and fix this. It never runs during playback:

  • frameHold=on: no input in the last 100 ms, so it is disarmed. Off and on behave identically.
  • frameHold=always: still held 0 of ~490 frames. Every pending frame was noRoom. The page's own work after its rAF callback is 4.8–5.5 ms (p50, 5.8–6.3 ms p90) during playback, so the page alone already reaches Chrome's ~5–6 ms limit. There is no time left to wait for the worker.

The cap the hold computes is vsync + limit (~5.1) − page work after hold (p90 ~5.8) − 0.3 ≈ vsync − 1 ms, which is already in the past when rAF runs, so it declines. Holding anyway would make the whole page miss its vsync, which is worse than a Vello repeat.

4. What makes it smooth

  • Render playback a frame ahead. With no input, the next frame's animation time is known. The worker renders frame k+1 as soon as frame k is pushed, so it is waiting long before the next draw: early every time, whatever the page does.
  • Stop the per-frame layout. Something re-lays out the whole page (766 objects, about half the time text shaping) every frame while the clock runs. Without it the page draws ~1–2 ms after vsync, the worker is late every time, and playback is smooth but one frame behind. That also gives the hold room during drags.
  • The small window was mostly smooth for the same reason: a shorter tick lands before the draw nearly every time.
