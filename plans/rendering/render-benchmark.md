# Render benchmark: what a 2D composition costs before culling or caching

**Status:** Measured 2026-10-13 on branch `document-spine`. SIMD baseline remeasured;
selection/hover geometry caching implemented and measured (§6). Picking, spatial
culling, and raster caching remain follow-ups.
How to run it is in `.claude/skills/run-iron/SKILL.md` § Render benchmark.

## 1. What was built

- **`?stress=N`** (or `await __iron.stress(N)`): a `stress` composition of N shapes in
  groups ("cells") of 100, all inside one `field` group, over an area several times a
  panel (`src/document_bridge/stress.rs`). 60% rects (a third stroked, a quarter
  rotated), 30% circles, 10% closed three-point curved paths. The field's rotation is
  bound to a `spin` slider. One fixed 20×20 `sample` rect sits in the first cell for
  dragging.
- **Phase timers** (`src/perf.rs`, read with `await __iron.timings(reset)`): CPU time of
  `sync`, `gesture_reconcile`, `display_list` (walking each 2D view's nodes into display
  lists), `extract` (copying changed lists to the render world), `encode` (Vello
  `prepare_scene`: flatten, tile and strip each changed view on the CPU), `render`
  (submitting it), `pick`, and whole worker frames: `frame` (every frame) and
  `changed_frame` (frames in which some view re-encoded: the cost of one change). Each
  phase records only frames on which it did work.
- **`bench-render.sh`** and **`bench-render-table.py`**: headless Chrome on the GPU,
  vsync and frame-rate limit off, the scenarios below, one table.
- **A `wasm-bench` cargo profile** (opt-level 3, no debug info). The usual `dev-opt`
  build is opt-level 1 and roughly triples `encode` (16.6 ms against 4.7 ms for 1,000
  shapes), so it is the wrong build to measure CPU cost with.

Scenarios, in the Stress tab, 1600×1000 window:

| Scenario | What changes | Which remedy it measures |
|---|---|---|
| `idle` | nothing; pointer over empty canvas | the per-frame floor |
| `hover-group` | nothing; pointer resting over the field group | the hover outline |
| `select-group` | nothing; the field group selected | the selection box |
| `spin` | the field's bound rotation: every shape moves, one leaf changes | subtree raster cache |
| `drag-one` | one rect inside a cell, Cmd-dragged | damage-driven re-encoding |
| `pan` | the view's pan, 5 px a step | culling, tiles |
| `*-zoomed` | the same at zoom ≈0.37, all content small | baking at low zoom |

## 3. What it says

1. **Two per-frame costs are bugs, not rendering, and they are the largest numbers in
   the table.** With a big group selected, every frame costs 11 ms at 1,000 shapes and
   119 ms at 10,000, with nothing changing. Hovering one costs 1.3 ms and 13 ms. The
   select overlay (`draw_select_overlay`, `bevy_app/select.rs`) recomputes, every
   frame, the group's box (`Geo::frame`, over its descendants' boxes) and, for a
   selection, its resize and rotate parts (`Geo::parts`, every leaf), and then asks each
   leaf for its handle (`Geo::resizable` → `free` → `Store::handle`) only to decide
   whether the grips draw white or grey. Its display list comes out identical, so nothing
   re-encodes; the walk is the cost, linear in descendants: about 12 µs each when
   selected, 1.3 µs when hovered. The fix is to recompute only when the selection, the
   hover or a node under them changed, and to read bounds from the `Bounds2d` the render
   pass already keeps.
2. **Picking runs every frame the pointer is over a canvas, over every node**: 1.6 ms at
   10,000 shapes, 3.3 ms at 20,000, idle. It only needs to run when the pointer moved or
   the document changed, and a spatial index (`understory_index`) would make each run
   proportional to what is under the pointer.
3. **Encode is half to two thirds of a changed frame, and it scales with every path, not
   only visible ones.** At zoom 1 about a sixth of the 10,000-shape field is on screen,
   yet encode is 13–14 ms; zoomed out with all of it on screen, 25–26 ms. Vello skips
   coverage work outside the view but still flattens every path it is given. So:
   - **Culling** (don't emit what can't touch the view) would cut zoom-1 encode and the
     display-list walk by most of their cost at 10,000 shapes, roughly halving a changed
     frame. It does nothing when everything is on screen.
   - **A subtree raster cache** is what helps the zoomed-out cases, which are also the
     most expensive: `spin-zoomed` (a whole group rotating) would become one textured
     quad, and `drag-one` would re-encode one cell of 100 shapes instead of 10,000.
4. **The rest of a changed frame is probably Bevy's per-entity work** (not timed
   directly). At 10,000 shapes, `spin` costs about 10 ms more than the timed phases add
   up to and `pan` about 2 ms. Rotating the field changes every entity's
   `GlobalTransform`, and Bevy propagates and checks all of them; panning changes none.
   A cached subtree would still pay this unless 2D shapes stop being one entity each,
   which is a larger question (the store could feed the display list directly).
5. **Budget.** A change stays within 60 fps up to about 5,000 shapes at zoom 1, not when
   zoomed out. 10,000 shapes run at 30–45 fps on a change, 20,000 at 15–25.

## 4. What to do, in order

1. **Fix the overlay recompute** (finding 1). Small, local, and the biggest win: a
   selected group should cost nothing per frame.
2. **Pick on change only** (finding 2), then with an index.
3. **Cull per view**, with the bounds already in `Bounds2d`, either directly or through
   `understory_index` (finding 3). Re-run the benchmark.
4. **Then the subtree raster cache**, starting with the case the numbers point at:
   static groups when zoomed out, invalidated by `DocumentChanges` (finding 3), using the
   offscreen view machinery from the view-textures work. Re-run, and compare
   `spin-zoomed` and `drag-one`.

## 5. Known gaps in the measurement

- **Measured without wasm SIMD.** These runs used the `wasm-bench` profile without
  `-Ctarget-feature=+simd128`, which the shipped build has; Vello's CPU stages then run
  a scalar fallback, so `encode` here is pessimistic. `build.sh` now adds the flag for
  `wasm-bench`. Re-measure before relying on the encode numbers; the overlay, picking
  and display-list findings don't involve Vello and stand.

- **CPU only.** GPU time shows only in frames per second, which also counts the idle
  frames between pointer events, so it is not reported per change.
- **The 5K run is not valid yet for pointer scenarios.** At device scale 2 the script's
  panel coordinates miss the slider, the sample and the group, so `spin`, `drag-one`,
  `hover-group` and `select-group` measured the wrong thing; only `idle` and `pan` mean
  anything there (10,000 shapes: `pan` changed frame 53 ms, encode 42 ms, against 22 and
  14 at 1600×1000 — encode cost follows device pixels). Document px appear to map to
  device px rather than CSS px at scale 2, which is worth checking on its own.
- **Timer resolution** is 0.1 ms in the worker (the page is not cross-origin isolated).
- Hover and selection outlines are recomputed every frame, so a scenario that leaves the
  pointer over content or a group selected contaminates every scenario after it. The
  script parks the pointer and deselects with paced Escapes, and prints `STILL
  SELECTED` if that fails.

### Implementation and measurement validity

- `SelectionGeometryCache` in `src/bevy_app/select.rs` shares composition-space
  selection frames, per-item frames, grip eligibility, and hover boxes between
  idle cursor testing and overlay drawing. Detailed gesture `Part`s are built
  on press. Pan/zoom is applied at use time.
- Keys include reaches, authored version, a non-drained bridge geometry generation,
  and playhead time. Previews/derived changes and same-version document loads
  invalidate the cache. Pending resolved changes bypass reuse. Entries made
  before transform propagation are finalized afterwards, since embed bounds can
  read Bevy globals.
- Invalidation is deliberately global for now: an unrelated transaction or
  playhead change may rebuild selected geometry. Per-subtree dependency revisions
  belong with the independent bounds subsystem. This does not make selected-group
  animation or the first large-group gesture constant-time.
- `overlay_geometry` times nonempty selection/hover cache rebuilds. It excludes
  emitting the small overlay list and empty-selection bookkeeping.
- Benchmark panel offsets now explicitly use physical pixels, divided by DPR for
  Playwright's CSS coordinates (`PANEL_PIXELS=physical`). The application's
  document-to-physical-pixel convention at zoom 1 is unchanged. The default
  driver's panel offsets remain CSS pixels for the existing smoke script.
- Browser checks at DPR 1 and DPR 2 verify that the corrected offsets hit the
  named field/sample and scrub the spin slider to 0.5. This validates coordinate
  mapping; it does not supply a new full 5K performance matrix.
- The driver supports `assert`, and command failures, page errors, or Rust panics
  fail the process. `bench-render.sh` uses pipefail and asserts selection identity
  and successful deselection instead of silently measuring the wrong target.

## 7. Change-driven picking, independent spatial bounds and culling (2026-10-13)

**Implemented in the working tree.** Comparison is against §6's selection-cache
implementation with SIMD already enabled. Same Apple M5 / ANGLE Metal protocol,
`wasm-bench`, 1600×1000 DPR 1, nine scenarios at 1k and 10k. Worker CPU p50 ms;
separate runs, not a controlled GPU latency measurement. Reading phase totals as
a sum of medians would be incorrect, because phases have different sample sets.

### Resting work

All four resting scenarios (`idle`, `hover-group`, `select-group`, `idle-zoomed`)
have `pick.n = 0`, `bounds.n = 0`, `bounds_updated = 0` and
`overlay_geometry.n = 0`. At 10k, resting worker-frame p50 drops from 2.7 to
0.9 ms; at 1k, 0.2–0.3 to 0.1 ms. The remaining idle cost is outside these
work phases. The 3D ray pass still runs each frame.

### Changed frames

| Shapes | Scenario | Before changed frame | After changed frame | Before encode | After encode | Bounds/index update |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | spin | 5.00 | 5.30 | 2.20 | 2.20 | 0.40 |
| 1,000 | drag-one | 4.00 | 4.00 | 2.10 | 2.10 | 0.00 |
| 1,000 | pan | 3.80 | 3.90 | 2.00 | 2.00 | 0.00 |
| 1,000 | spin-zoomed | 4.30 | 4.90 | 1.70 | 1.70 | 0.40 |
| 1,000 | pan-zoomed | 3.20 | 3.40 | 1.50 | 1.60 | 0.00 |
| 10,000 | spin | 27.00 | 17.90 | 10.20 | 2.10 | 4.40 |
| 10,000 | drag-one | 19.00 | 6.00 | 10.60 | 2.90 | 0.00 |
| 10,000 | pan | 18.70 | 5.30 | 10.40 | 2.50 | 0.00 |
| 10,000 | spin-zoomed | 32.60 | 32.70 | 14.90 | 13.70 | 4.30 |
| 10,000 | pan-zoomed | 22.90 | 19.90 | 14.30 | 13.10 | 0.00 |

At 10k zoom 1, pan improves by ~72%, a single-shape drag by ~68%, and group
rotation by ~34%. Pan's maximum encoded commands drop from 12,994 to 1,796;
rotating the field peaks at 2,015. A single-shape drag updates one bound;
rotation updates 10,002 (requested shapes, the extra sample and the slider).
The indexed exact-pick walk examines at most 3–7 candidates during these
10k changed scenarios instead of walking the whole tree. Candidate counts include
embedded recursion and describe a whole 2D walk; the diagnostic max is across
walks in the measurement interval.

At zoom ~0.37, most shapes are visible: pan still improves (~13%), but rotating
the entire 10k field is effectively unchanged (32.6→32.7 ms). Full descendants'
world bounds still need updating (4.3 ms), and visible paths still need encoding
(13.7 ms). At 1k there is little culling benefit, and index maintenance adds
~0.4 ms during whole-group motion; `spin-zoomed` rises 4.3→4.9 ms. Do not claim
that culling makes arbitrary rotate/scale constant-time or that it solves low-zoom
encoding. These results support proceeding later to retained overlay targets,
presentation chunks and selective subtree baking rather than background tiling.

### Implementation and the initial regression

- Bounds preparation runs after Bevy propagation and before render submission;
  hidden compositions participate. The index accepts resolved globals and Iron
  `NodeId` payloads, rather than adding another transform hierarchy.
- Separate geometry, painted and interaction boxes include local transformed
  stroke/miter margins, path-hit tolerance, slider-knob overhang and device-space
  AA expansion at the query. Current editor outlines still live in content, so
  their possible extent is conservatively included until overlay targets exist.
- Structural edits/load rebuild the indexes, retaining old painted damage.
  Nonstructural updates scan Bevy change flags and update changed leaves only.
  Old/new painted damage includes same-bounds style edits; it is diagnostic,
  not yet mapped into per-view partial raster invalidation.
- Canonical composition indexes are shared across occurrences. View/embedding
  clips map back conservatively; precise shape/clip/occlusion tests and exact
  painter order remain authoritative. `__iron.spatial(false)` selects the old
  exhaustive query/draw walks for correctness comparisons while bounds stay live.
- The first implementation used incremental tree updates for every moving leaf
  and sorted candidates twice using repeated map lookups. At 10k low zoom this
  regressed rotating frames to 42.1 ms and pan to 27.3 ms. Cached sort keys and
  avoiding a second single-scope sort reduce the display-list phase from ~10 ms
  to ~3 ms. Dense batches now use Understory's packed bulk builder; bounds update
  falls from ~6.1 to ~4.3 ms. Sparse edits preserve individual entries.
  The initial threshold is scopes ≥256 entries with over half moving; it is a
  tunable implementation policy, not an upstream or universally optimal constant.

Raw samples: [final implementation](benchmarks/render-spatial-after-2026-10-13.jsonl),
[initial incremental implementation](benchmarks/render-spatial-incremental-2026-10-13.jsonl),
and [prior cache baseline](benchmarks/render-selection-cache-after-2026-10-13.jsonl).

### Remaining work

Marquee/fit and exact oriented selected-group geometry still use existing `Geo`
queries. Shared subtree summaries and finer dependency revisions are separate
work. Dirty views still rebuild their whole visible display lists; editor
outlines still share content targets. No raster cache, presentation-chunk policy,
atlas or world-space tiles were added. The pinned tutorial inspiration and the
Understory ownership choices are recorded in
[infinite-canvas-architecture-patterns.md §13](../specs/infinite-canvas-architecture-patterns.md#13-implementation-checkpoint--change-driven-picking-and-spatial-foundation-2026-10-13).

## 8. Display-paced interaction and independent renderer checks

The development localhost artifact was substantially slower than the optimized
SIMD build behind §6–7. A new whole-selected-group test at 1k records ~19 FPS /
51 ms changed-frame CPU in dev-opt versus ~60 FPS / 11–12 ms optimized. The owner
confirmed smoother Helium interaction after reloading the optimized artifact.
The original nine-scenario matrix did not include whole-selected-group dragging.

See [renderer-sanity-checks.md](renderer-sanity-checks.md) for build/DPR controls,
5K worker/main/no-log comparisons, continuous rAF input verification, Canvas2D
and ThorVG replays of frozen Iron geometry, raw data, limitations and the next
standalone-Vello isolation experiment. FPS figures describe callback cadence;
no GPU-completion or input-to-photon claim is made. Optimized artifacts are now
left served deliberately, superseding §7's final dev-opt restoration note.


### All-changing geometry and standalone Vello control

The follow-up in `renderer-sanity-checks.md` §7 now includes a Bevy-free, same-
version Vello Hybrid 0.2 replay. All 10k leaves move independently and change
rectangle proportions, circle radii or cubic curvature every frame. Canvas2D
recreates every Path2D; ThorVG resets paths; Vello resets one complete scene.
No custom raster cache is used. At the matched 2040×1490 target / zoom 0.37,
Canvas2D, ThorVG GL and Vello GL/WG sustain ~60-Hz callback cadence. Vello GL
synchronous work is ~13.8 ms p50 / 14.2 ms p95, versus Canvas2D ~6.4 / 7.2 ms.
The selected-group all-leaf outline case costs Vello GL ~36.5 ms, even without
Bevy. This is a concrete feedback-rendering bottleneck, separate from document
propagation and selection-derived geometry. CPU submission/callback cadence is
not GPU completion, displayed-frame proof, or a complete-editor 10k guarantee.
Forty final intervals and version/GPU metadata are retained in
`benchmarks/render-dynamic-controls-2026-10-13.jsonl`.

## 9. Retained overlay target and frame-only group outlines (2026-10-14)

`handoff-render.md` §4 item 1. Editor feedback (lit outlines, the selection's
box and grips, path points, marquee) now draws into one `Overlay` view per 2D
panel, a texture of its own composited above the panel's (`vector::Overlay`,
z 0.6 over 0.5). The content walk no longer reads highlights in panels, and
painted bounds lose their 4 px outline allowance. Group policy, following Figma
(owner's decision): a lit leaf draws its outline, a lit group or embed draws its
frame, never one stroke per descendant. `Highlights` holds lit nodes only; 3D
meshes and plane views light under a lit ancestor (`Highlights::covers`).
The timers split `encode` (content) from the new `overlay_encode`, and the
matrix gains `hover-sweep` (pointer swept on and off the field four times).

Same protocol as §7: `wasm-bench` with SIMD, 1600×1000 DPR 1, worker CPU p50 ms.

to an embed's region (Figma shows them past a clipping frame too). Plane views,
which have no overlay, still stroke lit nodes in their content.

## 10. Group-local spatial scene: a moved group is one entry (2026-10-17)

`handoff-render.md` §4 item 3's spatial half; phase 1 of
[`../vision/10_engine-boundary.md`](../vision/10_engine-boundary.md). The store
now classifies what moved (`geom::Revisions`: a node's own **placement**, its
**local** geometry, or **structure**), and `iron_spatial::Scene` keeps one index
per group in the group's local space with one placement affine per group.
Rotating the stress field rewrites the field's entry in its parent's index and
nothing below it; a leaf edit rewrites the leaf and, only if a union grew, its
ancestors. The bridge's `prepare_spatial` reads no Bevy transform any more
(`Bounds2d` is gone); drawing and picking still take placements from Bevy
globals until phase 2, and the store-versus-Bevy drift check stays on.

Measured on a Linux container with SwiftShader only (4 cores, no GPU), so the
app ran at 1–5 fps and the GPU protocol of §6–9 could not be repeated; frame
times below are not comparable with the M5 rows above. The bounds phase and
the entry counts are, since they are CPU work that does not depend on the
renderer. `wasm-bench` with SIMD, 1600×1000 DPR 1, worker CPU p50 ms; the
phase timer's resolution is 0.1 ms.

The container baseline is commit `6ab558d` with only the stress id fix applied,
measured by a reduced script (`idle`, `spin`, `pan`; the full matrix's
Cmd-click on the sample does not land at 1–5 fps). Its 10k `spin` changed frame
was 84 ms against 67 ms after; what remains is Bevy's propagation over one
entity per node and the encode, both untouched here.

Native, release, `iron_spatial/tests/scene.rs` (`ten_thousand_shapes_rotate_as_one_entry`),
the stress shape (100 cells of 100): rotate update 24 µs, a leaf drag 25 µs
(one entry when the cell's union still covers it, up to three when it grows),
a 1600×1000 visible-rect query 142 µs for 405 hits, full rebuild on a
structural change 40 ms.

What this does not change yet: the `overlay_geometry` cost of a selected-group
drag (the selection cache still keys on the global `GeometryRevision`; item 3's
other half), Bevy's own propagation over one entity per node (phase 2 removes it
for document nodes), and encoding. Candidates after a rotation are a superset
of a flat index's: a group's query box is the rotated query's bounding box in
the group's space, so the hosts' exact tests (shape containment, the view clip)
decide, as they already did.

Verification: `iron_document` and `iron_spatial` native suites (six new scene
tests, including a flat-reference equivalence when nothing is rotated and a
superset-of-exact check after rotation); the full smoke; `spatial.txt` at DPR 1
and 2 with identical reference/indexed pixels in all four pairs and 36 of 37
assertions (the through-plane step reads a fixed pixel that depends on the
panel geometry; it was checked by hand at this machine's position in both
modes); `picking.txt` 10 of 10. The full smoke's small value drifts against SKILL.md's
expected output (a slider scrub reading 0.76, a drag stopping short) reproduce
with the baseline build on this container, where driver-paced input runs ahead
of ~8 fps frames. Raw samples:
[`render-scene-local-2026-10-17.jsonl`](benchmarks/render-scene-local-2026-10-17.jsonl).
Two fixes landed alongside: `?stress=N` panicked since paths gained a contour
node (one id short per path), and `spatial.txt`'s fixture markup predated the
current edit grammar.
