    Part 6 of 14 · Rendering

    Every view is a texture

    Embedding needed one thing the renderer couldn't do: draw a composition into something other than the window. Fixing that turned into a rewrite of how everything reaches the screen. Every view (a 2D panel, a 3D camera, a 3D view inside a 2D composition, a 2D composition on a plane in 3D) now renders into its own texture, sized to where it's seen, and only when something it shows changed. This part follows plans/specs/view-textures.md: the problem, the model, the three uses, render on demand, and the rendering order, retained chunks, raster tiles and instance sharing.

    You needPart 5's panels and embeds; the idea of a Bevy camera and a render target.
    Filessrc/vector/{backend,hybrid,view_composite,display_list}.rs, bevy_app/scene3d.rs (ViewImage, sync_view_images), document_bridge/planes.rs, src/lib.rs (RedrawNeeded, WorkerApp::frame).

    6.1The problem, from the source

    How it was

    Every camera paid for the whole window, every frame. Bevy allocates each camera's colour and depth buffers at the size of its render target, the window, and a viewport only restricts where it draws. A 3D panel a fifth of the window paid for full-window buffers; at 5K, 5120×2880 each. The 2D side merged every layer into one window-sized Vello scene, cleared and redrawn each frame. So GPU cost scaled with window area times panel count, an idle frame wasn't free, and a composition couldn't be drawn into anything but the shared screen.

    The model

    A view is one source seen one way, rendered into its own texture. The texture is sized to the view's footprint on screen, kept between frames, and re-rendered only when something it shows changed. A texture has three possible uses: pasted 1:1 into a panel by the window's one camera, drawn inside a 2D view under an affine transform, or sampled on a plane in 3D.

    The plan's own summary of why this also enables everything else in Parts 5 and 9: once each view has a texture, "show this composition inside that one" is "draw that texture there".

    6.2Vector views

    A 2D layer is an entity with a DisplayList (the renderer-independent list of fills and strokes the bridge draws) and a VectorLayer. Its view selects the target texture; painter sub-order, affine placement and cache key let multiple chunk layers share that target.

    Each 2D composition panel spawns one entity that is both a layer and a VectorView over the panel's rectangle (render.rs:49). Editor feedback (the selection box, grips, hover outlines, the marquee) has its own Overlay view stacked above each panel's content view since October 14, so a hover or selection change re-encodes only that small overlay texture and never the content beneath it.

    The backend gives every view a texture and a quad that pastes it into the window. Sizes round up to 64 px buckets, so dragging a pane divider rarely reallocates:

    Each target keeps its own retained Vello scene and remembers whether its texture already holds it. When no layer of a view changed, the view isn't rendered at all; its texture is still on the GPU and the window pastes it again:

    Pasting texel for pixel

    The window has exactly one camera now. It clears to the background and draws every view's quad with ViewCompositeMaterial, whose shader computes the texture coordinate from the fragment's window position, so the mapping is exact by construction:

    Two encodings arrive. Vello writes premultiplied, sRGB-encoded bytes into a UNORM texture; a Bevy camera writes straight alpha into an sRGB texture. The material's mode tells the shader which it's reading, so neither gets its colours shifted.

    6.33D views

    A 3D panel's camera renders into its own texture instead of the window, with the same bucketing and the same retention. ViewImage tracks the texture and when the camera needs to run:

    sync_view_images marks a camera active only when something it shows changed: its rectangle, its pose or lens, or any 3D content on its render layers moving, highlighting, appearing or disappearing. Otherwise it sleeps and its texture is reused. Two practical details from getting this right: a camera woken for one frame can render with stale per-view state (lights, visibility), so it stays awake for a few frames after a change; and the system runs before Bevy's frustum and visibility systems, so a camera woken this frame is culled and lit as an active view this frame.

    Camera orders (Bevy needs them unique) come from the lowest free slot, so cameras appearing and disappearing never collide (scene3d.rs:270).

    3D inside 2D

    A 3D embed's camera renders into a texture sized to the embed's on-screen size (Part 5's EmbedView), and the 2D view that contains the embed draws that texture with DrawCmd::Texture, under the embed's whole transform, in painter order. So a 3D view inside a 2D composition can be rotated, scaled, clipped and drawn over like any other shape. Vello can't read a Bevy camera's sRGB texture directly, so each one is converted into Vello's encoding first (Convert, hybrid.rs:1015).

    Picking goes the other way: PointerToCamera stores the map from window pixels to the embed camera's own texture pixels, through every transform on the way, so clicking the torus inside a rotated embed casts the ray from the right place.

    2D on a plane in 3D

    A 2D composition embedded in 3D is a plane mesh whose material samples an offscreen vector view: a VectorView marked Offscreen, rendered but never pasted into the window. Its resolution comes from how large the plane appears: project the plane's corners through every camera that sees it, and take the largest screen-space length of one document pixel:

    Densities step by powers of √2, so a slow camera dolly changes the texture size rarely. This is the first piece of the user's "tiled, demand-driven textures" extension (plan §4): views are already described as a content-space region at a density, and demand is already computed per use. The plane remains a single offscreen view texture; the cold-chunk raster cache below is a separate tiling mechanism for vector content.

    6.4Render on demand

    With every view retaining its texture, a frame where nothing visible changed needs no GPU work at all. WorkerApp::frame runs the main world, asks whether anything the window shows changed, and only then runs extraction and rendering:

    Changed display lists, views, cameras, panel geometry and assets wake rendering, with a short linger for settling. Raster work keeps rendering until required tiles are ready. Free-running compositions are an explicit exception: Auto follows live sources and embeds, Always runs continuously, and On demand suppresses continuous updates. The Stats composition is itself a live source, so an idle experiment must first set Stats to On demand.

    6.5Two latency fixes

    After the migration you reported more lag on your display. Headless pacing was a steady 16.7 ms in every case, so the report wasn't reproduced, but reading the schedule turned up two real one-frame delays:

    1. 2D could reach the window a frame late. Vello's render_scene and Bevy's render_system (which runs every camera and composites the window) were in the same render phase with no order between them. If Vello ran second, the window pasted last frame's 2D. Vello is explicitly ordered first.
    2. 3D inside 2D was a frame behind its camera. The 2D view sampled the 3D texture before Bevy had rendered this frame's image. Views that draw a 3D texture now render inside the window camera's own schedule, after every 3D camera and before the composite, in the same GPU submission (the camera-local render pass). Each such view has its own Vello renderer, because a renderer's queued buffer writes all land before the submission, and two views sharing one would overwrite each other.

    6.6The current pipeline: retained chunks

    The old pipeline rebuilt a whole panel's display list and re-encoded its whole scene when one visible shape changed. document_bridge/chunks.rs now cuts a composition into painter-contiguous pieces. A chunk owns an immutable shared Recorded command list, drawn through each panel's pan and zoom. Ordinary chunks are shared across views; chunks containing embeds record per panel because the sampled texture belongs to an occurrence.

    The target cost is 2,000 commands, counting complex paths by their segments. Large subtrees split along sibling runs; isolated opacity, clipping, nodes that draw with children and embeds impose boundaries so painter order and compositing stay correct. Structural changes can repartition; value changes record affected chunks. Translation of a large split group changes chunk frames and moves cached tiles rigidly. Rotation or scale changes its basis and records those chunks again.

    Bevy 0.20 / wgpu 30 / vello_gpu 0.3 provide the current backend. The classic bevy_vello path is gone. Per-view textures and the independent overlay are still the outer model; caching happens inside them.

    6.7Cold content becomes tiles, hot content stays live

    vector/raster.rs groups consecutive cold cacheable chunks, with hot chunks drawing live between them. A changed recording stays hot for 0.5 seconds. Texture, filter and backdrop-dependent content cannot bake; cheap views remain live. The initial raster threshold is 2,000 visible commands, with a lower 1,000-command threshold for switching back.

    Each atlas slot is 256×256 texels: 254×254 content with a one-texel gutter. Atlas pages are 2048² RGBA8, under a default 256 MiB budget. During zoom or fractional translation, existing tiles stretch up to 2×; after motion settles, exact scale and subpixel phase tiles replace them. A missing or damaged square uses another suitable tiling or draws live, so incomplete baking never leaves a hole or stale pixels. Baking prioritizes visible squares and budgets background work at 6 ms per frame.

    Changes damage only the tiles covered by changed commands. Regrouping keeps the old coverage until replacements are ready, with live drawing over damaged regions. __iron.raster(false) provides the uncached comparison; __iron.timings() and the Render inspector expose recording, tile and live-layer work.

    6.8Equal instances share drawings

    iron_document::analysis follows each picture part's dependencies separately for shape, placement and effects. A channel is static if it depends on no input, keyed if it depends only on constant arguments or overrides, and live if it reaches a wired input, runtime producer, constraint or outside dependency. Painter-contiguous runs become layers. Equal static/keyed runs share recordings and raster tiles across instances; a moving needle stays live while its meter face stays baked.

    Repeated SVG pieces use lightweight references that draw definition templates without an expanded picture tree. This reduced the measured Prussia import from 42,667 to 14,407 nodes. The recorded near-whole drag fell from 235.5 to 0.6 ms after rigid chunk placement; the earlier raster pass reduced a fitted pan from 177 to 1.2 ms. These are particular benchmark results, not guarantees for every file: plans/rendering/render-benchmark.md and plans/specs/instance-cache.md retain the fixtures and conditions.

    What remains

    Instance expansion still has a whole-document wanted-set pass. Live vector retention is a measurement-driven follow-up; it is distinct from the shared command recordings and raster cache already built. The window composite still runs for rendered frames, and 2D-on-3D plane textures have no mipmaps. GPU encoding cost, frame scheduling and actual display presentation are different costs; Part 11 explains native pacing and its measurement limits.
