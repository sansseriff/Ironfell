    Constraints and the solver

    Bindings run one way: the bar reads the slider. Many relationships in a diagram run both ways: two joints are coincident, and whichever one you drag, the other follows. This part covers roadmap stages C2 and D: a small numerical solver crate, constraint nodes in the document, the clusters they form, and the pendulum demo that drags through them.

    You needPart 3: handles, previews, begin_drag/drag_to, and what a driver is.
    Filessrc/iron_solve/src/{lib,cluster,analysis,linalg}.rs, iron_document/src/{constraint,solve,lib}.rs, the pendulum in document_bridge/demo.rs. Design: plans/constraint-system.md.

    4.1Why bindings aren't enough

    The simple version

    Bind everything to one master. Make joint 2's position = joint 1 + 100·(cos θ, sin θ), and so on down the chain. Dragging the ball now has no handle: its position is bound to three angles, non-linearly. You can only drag the master.

    What was built

    Constraints without a direction. A distance constraint between two joints says only that they stay 100 px apart. At every solve, whatever is being dragged is a driver, every other free position is a variable, and a numerical solver finds the nearest pose where every constraint holds.

    4.2iron_solve: a solver that knows nothing about documents

    The solver is its own crate with no dependencies. It knows variables and residual functions, nothing about nodes or constraint kinds. That separation is deliberate: the adapter in iron_document translates, so the numerical method could be swapped without touching the document.

    A problem is a vector of variables, a scale per variable, and terms. A term is a small function over a few variables that writes residuals: numbers that are zero when the term is satisfied.

    The three tiers do different jobs:

    TierMeansExample
    RequiredMust hold after a successful solveTwo joints coincide; a link is 100 px long
    StrongSatisfy as far as the required terms allowThe ball is under the pointer; a transaction set x = 900
    WeakA preferenceStay where you were (added automatically, one per variable)

    The weak "stay where you were" rows are what make dragging feel physical: of the infinitely many poses that satisfy the constraints and put the ball under the pointer, the solver picks the one closest to the last frame, so elbows don't flip.

    The method: projected Levenberg–Marquardt

    The solver minimises the weighted sum of squared residuals. Each iteration linearises every term (central finite differences, only over each term's own variables), solves a damped least-squares step, and then projects the trial pose back onto the required terms with a few Newton steps before judging it:

    Plainly: try a step; if the cost went down, accept it and trust the model more (λ shrinks); if not, damp harder (λ grows ×10) and try again. The projection means the iteration walks along the surface where the constraints hold, rather than trading constraint error against the pointer through a penalty weight. A finger the pendulum can't reach leaves a strong residual equal to the gap, and the chain points straight at the pointer at full extension.

    Reading the numerics

    normal_equations forms JᵀJ and Jᵀr from the Jacobian J and residuals r. cholesky_solve solves (JᵀJ + λI)δ = −Jᵀr. Variables are divided by their scale first, so a rotation (radians, scaled by the node's half-diagonal) and a position (pixels) are comparable. All loops run in index order without hashing, so the same input gives bit-identical output, which keeps tests and undo deterministic.

    Clusters and analysis

    Terms that share no variables are independent problems. cluster.rs groups them with union-find, so dragging the pendulum never re-solves an unrelated mechanism:

    analysis.rs answers "how many ways can this still move?" (degrees of freedom) and, when a system can't be solved, "which constraints are to blame?". It computes the rank of the required Jacobian, then drops one term at a time and re-tests (SolveSpace's method). That's where the error message likely at fault: #c3, #c4 comes from.

    4.3Constraints in the document

    A constraint is an ordinary node: a constraint component (kind, value, enabled) and one constrains relation per operand. The relation's data carries the operand's role and its anchor, a point in the operand's local space:

    The vocabulary is code, like node types. A value can be bound: the pendulum's three distance constraints read value = #length.slider.value · 100, a driving dimension. Validation rejects malformed constraints and duplicates (two constraints of one kind over the same anchored operands) with an explicit error, because the main author of constraints is going to be a model, and an error teaches more than silent merging.

    Every residual is written in world positions (pose.point(v, anchor), which uses Part 3's world transforms), so a joint inside a rotated group is constrained where it actually is.     4.5When the solver runs

    Two moments, with different rules for what is written:

    During a dragWhen a transaction applies
    Called fromresolve_clusters_live, after every previewpost_apply, after every transaction
    DriversFingersThe transaction's own Sets
    WritesPreviews (the overlay)Set ops, recorded as the transaction's derived_ops
    On failureKeep the last good poseReject the transaction, naming the culprits

    Solve-on-apply is what keeps the document consistent no matter who edits it. A model edit that sets the ball's x to 900 is a driver; the solver moves the joints, and their new positions are recorded as derived ops on the same transaction, so one undo puts everything back:

    Two refinements matter for the model. The returned SolverSummary reports causes, not every changed value: which clusters re-solved, which nodes moved, and any request the solver couldn't honour (<unmet>). And exact: true on a transaction turns an unmet request into a rejection instead of an adjustment, for edits that must land exactly or not at all.

    Because apply runs post_apply on the committed document and puts the old one back on error, a transaction that makes the constraints unsatisfiable never happens at all (lib.rs:697).

    4.6The pendulum

    The demo that exercises all of it lives in the Pendulum tab (and in Main, through its embed; Part 5):

    Dragging the ball places a finger on it; every frame the cluster re-solves and the joints move; the arms, being bindings over the joints' solved positions, follow in the same pass; the height bar follows the ball. Dragging an arm does nothing, with the reason logged: its position is bound to four free leaves, so it has no handle (Part 3). Moving the fix from the platform to the ball (a model edit) turns the mechanism around: now the platform drags and the ball stays put. Each frame logs solve: cluster #V, 12 vars, N iterations, M evaluations, T ms; T is well under the 2 ms budget.

    Rough edge

    After dragging the platform toward its reach limit, the committed pose is sometimes a hair short of the solver's fixed point. The next unrelated transaction then re-solves and nudges the platform by a fraction of a pixel, which shows up in the smoke log as "link" by Human: cluster #V re-solved; moved 1 node(s): #W. Harmless, and documented in SKILL.md, but it's solver tolerance leaking into history. A second open item: iron_solve's test three_link_chain_reaches_a_target fails at its 50-iteration cap, noted in constraint-system.md §14.
