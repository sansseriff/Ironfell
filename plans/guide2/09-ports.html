    Ports, connections and physics

    A laser feeds a fiber, which feeds a power meter; a heater warms a block through a thermal link. Entities need a way to be wired together so that a value computed in one flows into another. This part covers roadmap stage F, steps 1–3: port kinds, connections, one-way signals that ride the binding graph, across/through pairs solved as equations, and the two demos (Optics and Thermal) that use them.

    You needPart 2 (definitions, instantiate), Part 3 (the reactive graph, handles), Part 4 (iron_solve).
    Filesiron_document/src/port.rs, {component,definition,graph,handle,op,view,lib}.rs, document_bridge/demo.rs (optics, thermal), tests/ports.rs. Design: plans/entity-composition-model.md §6.1.

    9.1The decision: two kinds of variable, one interface

    You asked whether ports should carry constraint bundles or bidirectional signals. The answer recorded in ECM §6.1 is both, through one general interface: a port kind declares a bundle of variables, and each variable is one of two sorts.

    One-way signal

    out → in. Optical power along a fiber. The in-port's value is its partner out-port's value. It compiles to a dataflow edge in the binding graph, evaluated like any binding; cycles are rejected. A back-reflection would be a second signal running the other way.

    Across / through pair

    Acausal, as in Modelica, Simscape and SPICE. Temperature and heat flow; voltage and current. At a joint the across values are equal and the through values sum to zero. These compile to equations, solved together with each part's own behaviour equations. Loops are fine.

    Two things are deliberately not port features. Editing from either end (drag the meter's reading, and the laser's power moves) is the store's existing gesture machinery from Part 3: the meter's signal has a linear handle back to the laser. And geometry stays out of ports: a mount bolted to a table is a constraint (Part 4), not a connection. Physics flows through ports; placement goes through constraints.

    A definition (Part 2) gains two kinds of child: port_spec and equation. An out-port's spec carries its behaviour as a binding: the laser's out.signal is bound to its power parameter; the fiber's is its input attenuated by length and loss. instantiate copies each spec onto the new entity as a port, and each equation as an equation, with every reference rewritten the same way template bindings are:

    So #fiber_out.port.signal = #fiber_in.port.signal · 10^(−loss·length/10) in the definition becomes the same expression over the fiber entity's own ports and params. Templates read ports too: the meter's gauge width is bound to #meter_in.port.signal / 1 mW · 24, and place_figures maps each spec to the entity's port of the same name, so the schematic's gauge reads the same port.

    9.3Connections

    A connection is a connection node under the model root with exactly two connects relations, tagged end: a and end: b. That's all a file stores; nothing about which value flows where.

    One-way signals are derived bindings

    The simple version

    Write a binding onto the in-port when the connection is made. Now the file holds the wiring twice (the connection and the binding), deleting the connection must find and remove the binding, and an undo that restores one without the other leaves a ghost.

    What was built

    Derive the binding from the connection, never store it. Every time the graph is rebuilt, each live, valid one-way connection contributes one binding: in-port's signal reads out-port's signal. Delete the connection and the binding is simply not derived next time.

    The reactive graph (Part 3) folds them in alongside the stored bindings, so a signal is evaluated in topological order with everything else, in the same pass:

    This is what makes the chain feel instant. Move the laser-power slider: the entity's params.power (bound to the slider) changes, then the laser's out.signal, the fiber's in.signal (derived), the fiber's out.signal, the meter's in.signal (derived), and both gauges, all in one glitch-free pass.

    9.4Across/through pairs as equations

    A pair can't be a binding, because nothing says which way it runs: a thermal link relates two temperatures and a heat flow, and which one is "the answer" depends on what's held fixed elsewhere. So pairs become equations, and the solver from Part 4 finds the values.

    Assembling the network

    network collects every pair port on a live entity. Each port's across and through is an unknown unless it's bound. Ports joined by connections are grouped into nets by union-find (the same trick iron_solve uses for clusters); each net contributes its conservation laws; each live equation on an entity contributes lhs − rhs:

    Line 273 skips equations on definitions: a definition's equations describe the template, which has no network of its own. An unconnected terminal ends up in a net of one, where "throughs sum to zero" means it carries no flow, which is the physically right default for an open end.

    Solving, and where the answer lives

    Every equation becomes a Required term (Part 4's tiers). Linear rows are plain sums; Expr rows evaluate both sides with the unknowns substituted (as quantities with their dimensions, so 2 K/W · 5 W is a temperature) and everything else read from the store. The previous solution is the starting point, so a slider drag converges in a step or two.

    The solution is not written to the document. It goes into a new layer of the reactive graph, physics, which readers see beneath bindings:

    OrderLayerComes fromStored?

    So the heater's gauge, bound to (#out.port.across − 293.15 K) / 1 K · 6, reads the solved temperature like any value, and when the solution changes, set_physics invalidates its dependents like any change. resolve_physics runs after every transaction, on load, and when the playhead moves (lib.rs:654). A solved leaf is Locked for editing, with the reason "solved by its across/through network; change a source or a parameter instead" (handle.rs:46).

    Why not solve-on-apply like constraints?

    Constraint solutions (Part 4) are positions: authored state the user owns, so they are written as derived ops and undone with the transaction. Physics solutions are consequences: the temperature is whatever the heater's power and the link's resistance make it. Storing it would put a cache in the file that could disagree with its own inputs. A network that fails to converge keeps its previous solution and reports why through physics_error(); it never rejects a transaction.

    Rough edges and what's next

    The module comment at the top of port.rs still says pair solving "comes next"; it has since landed. There's no gesture for making a connection: they come from the demo or a model edit, and the schematic's wires are ordinary paths that don't follow their blocks. Pair networks solve as one problem; splitting them into independent clusters is easy (the nets already are) but unneeded at demo scale. Time-varying signals (a thermal mass, a modulated laser) would make the network a differential system, which is roadmap stage G and the subject of the open questions about animation and state machines.
